# Referee voice guide

Distilled writing conventions for referee reports and editor letters, for use as a
voice reference when drafting. **Voice rules and short anonymized fragments only —
no raw report text, no manuscript identifiers, no other papers' content.**

## Report structure

- **Open with a plain-language summary.** Name the submission in the third person
  ("X and coworkers have submitted a manuscript entitled …"), state what the paper
  does in one short paragraph, and give an early, unambiguous overall verdict.
- **Commit early.** If the paper is promising but flawed, say so plainly and add the
  standing offer: *"If the authors make satisfactory improvements, I will argue for
  its acceptance."* Don't bury the disposition.
- **Frame contributions as a list, then what's _notably not_ on it.** A recurring
  move: enumerate what the paper delivers, then "notably _not_ on the list are …" —
  this is where the real critique lands.
- **Group comments under `>`-prefixed headers**, in this order when applicable:
  `> Overall comment:` · `> Major issues:` · `> Moderate issues:` ·
  `> Flow of writing:` / `> Organization:` · `> Minor / typos:` · `> Closing:`.
- **Number major issues** `*1*`, `*2*`, … Moderate issues continue the numbering or
  restart under their own header. Keep each to a few sentences: claim → evidence
  (with a specific location) → the ask.

## Tone and phrasing

- **Skeptical but constructive; support gated on evidence.** The default posture for
  an unproven or approximate claim is earned doubt — "I remain doubtful … but will
  support once the evidence is there." Never hostile.
- **Reserve bare imperatives for genuine flaws.** For everything else, phrase the ask
  as an invitation: *"The authors may consider …"*, *"It would be helpful if the
  authors could …"*, or a direct question (*"Is the agreement sensitive to …?"*).
- **Emphasis via `_underscores_`** for key qualifiers (e.g. _very_ weak, _visually_),
  and `*asterisks*` sparingly for a sharp point.
- **Always cite a specific location** for a factual claim — page, equation, figure,
  or section (e.g. "(§VI.C, p. 16)", "Eq. (89)", "Fig. 7"). No unanchored assertions.
- **Close warmly.** End on the paper's merit and a forward look: acknowledge the
  hard problem, restate that the requests aim to align claims with evidence, and say
  you look forward to the revision. A congratulatory sentence is welcome when earned.

## Editor letter (separate from the report)

- **Keep it short** — a few sentences to a page; editors skim.
- **State the disposition up front** (e.g. "I lean toward acceptance after major
  revision") and make any doubt clearly _evidence-gated_.
- **When the editor asks structured questions, answer in the same a/b/c/d structure**,
  each item led by a scannable verdict word (YES / PARTLY / NOT YET / NO), then one
  or two sentences. Mirror the journal's own rating axes when relevant.
- The editor letter may be frank about venue fit and "landmark" calibration in ways
  the author-facing report is not.

## Anti-patterns to avoid

- Throat-clearing and meta-commentary ("I want to be clear that …", "as stated
  above"). Cut it.
- Stacking em-dashes; prefer periods.
- Vague asks ("improve the writing"); always say _what_ and _where_.
- Overstating certainty on an approximate result; keep verdicts calibrated.
